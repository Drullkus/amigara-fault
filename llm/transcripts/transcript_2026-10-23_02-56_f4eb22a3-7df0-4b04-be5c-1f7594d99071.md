### Claude:

The plan's depth section already describes this chain, so here is how to build it step by step. You wire; I describe.

**1. Find out what the depth pixels hold.** Put a Render Select TOP after the FreenectTD TOP and set its index to 1 for depth. Click the viewer-active toggle on it and hover over your body. The pixel readout at the bottom of the viewer shows the value. Middle-click the operator for its pixel format. You will see one of two cases:

- 32-bit float with values in the hundreds or thousands. That is millimeters. Use millimeters in every threshold below.
- Values between 0 and 1. The plugin already normalized it. Use the readout at known distances to learn the scale.

Pixels with no return read 0. Remember that for step 3.

**2. Decide your working range.** You own these two numbers: Near and Far in meters. The plan defaults to 2.5 and 6.5 for the show, but for a desk test use something like 0.8 and 2.0 so only you pass. Add them as custom parameters on the component later, so no magic numbers sit inside the network.

**3. Make the mask with two Threshold TOPs and a Composite TOP.**

- Threshold TOP A: input is the depth. Comparator "Greater", Threshold = Near. This also removes the zero pixels with no return.
- Threshold TOP B: same input. Comparator "Less", Threshold = Far.
- Composite TOP: inputs A and B, Operation "Multiply". White where both pass. That is your silhouette.

**4. Clean it.** Blur TOP with a small filter size, then another Threshold TOP at 0.5. This fills pinholes and smooths the ragged edge from time-of-flight noise. Then a Cache TOP so a dropped Kinect frame does not blink the mask.

**5. Make the depth output.** Level TOP on the raw depth. On its Range page, map Near to Far into 0 to 1, and clamp on the Clamp page. Multiply by the mask with another Composite TOP so the wall reads as 0.

**6. Outline, if you want a literal edge.** An Edge TOP on the mask gives a one-pixel outline. The holes shader later wants the filled mask, not the edge, so keep this as a preview branch.

Two things to know while you test:

- Depth registration maps the 512x424 depth onto the color camera's pixel grid. Turn it on in the FreenectTD TOP now, so the mask lines up with the RGB image you will use for calibration.
- Time-of-flight loses dark clothing and hair at range. If your head drops out, lower the blur threshold a little rather than widening Near and Far.

Given your shader background, the whole of steps 3 to 5 is one GLSL TOP: read `sTD2DInputs[0]`, take `uNear` and `uFar` as uniforms, output `step(near, d) * step(d, far)` in red and the remapped depth in green. That keeps one operator and lives in `td/shaders/` under Git. Tell me which route you want for `depth.tox`, and I will write the GLSL file if you pick the shader.

Current progress in the pic. However the edge is still grainy, per depth input. I'm wondering if something like Photoshop's median will help clean it up, unless there's a better-proven method for it.

### Claude:

Yes, a median is the right instinct, and it belongs before the threshold, not after. Your blur runs on the binary mask, so by then the grain is already baked in as holes and spurs. Three proven fixes, in the order I would try them:

**1. Check the plugin's own filters first.** libfreenect2 ships a bilateral filter and an edge-aware filter on the depth pipeline, and FreenectTD may expose them as toggles on the Freenect TOP. Look through its parameter pages. If they are off, turning them on is free and removes most speckle at the source.

**2. Average a few frames of depth before thresholding.** Time-of-flight noise is mostly temporal, so each pixel jitters around its true distance. A Cache Blend TOP on the raw depth, blending the last 2 to 4 frames, steadies the edge far more than any spatial filter. Then threshold. The cost is slight ghosting on fast motion, which is fine for a silhouette. Put this between `renderselect1` and `thresh1`.

**3. Spatial median on the depth.** TouchDesigner has no Median TOP, but a 3x3 median is a ten-line GLSL TOP, and you already know that shader. Insert it after the blend and before the threshold. A median keeps the edge sharp where a blur smears it, which is why it beats your current blur for this job.

Then, for the mask itself, replace the single blur with a morphological open and close. TouchDesigner lacks erode and dilate operators, but a Blur followed by a Threshold does the job: threshold high, around 0.7, erodes and kills spurs; threshold low, around 0.3, dilates and fills holes. One pair of each, with small blur sizes, gives a cleaner edge than one blur at 0.5. Keep `cache1` at the end for dropped frames.

One thing I see in the network: there is only one threshold, so you have a Near but no Far. Add the "Less than Far" threshold and multiply when you move from the desk to the wall, or everything behind you joins the mask.

If you want the median as a file in `td/shaders/` under Git, say so and I will write it with the radius as a uniform.

### Claude:

I cloned the plugin's source and read it, so both answers are grounded in the code.

**1. The filters are already on.** The plugin only sets `MinDepth` and `MaxDepth` on the libfreenect2 config and leaves the rest at defaults, and libfreenect2 defaults both the bilateral filter and the edge-aware filter to on. A mod could only turn them off, so there is nothing to gain. The grain you see is what the sensor gives after those filters.

Two plugin parameters are worth using instead, on the Freenect TOP's first page:

- "Manual Depth Range" with "Depth Range Min (mm)" and "Depth Range Max (mm)". With "Depth Output" left at Normalized, the depth texture is 16-bit, 0 to 1 across that window, and no-reading pixels get the "Unknown Depth Value", default 0. So Near and Far can live in the plugin, and your thresholds work in 0 to 1. Set Depth Output to Meters if you want 32-bit float meters instead.
- "Format": Raw, Raw Undistorted, or Registered. Registered is the one the plan wants for calibration.

**2. Your build has no Cache Blend TOP, so build the average by hand.** Two options. Both work better on the mask than on the depth, because the zero pixels with no reading would drag a depth average toward the camera, while averaging a binary mask is just a vote.

- Exponential average with a Feedback TOP. Place a Feedback TOP fed by `thresh1`. Place a Cross TOP with input 1 from the Feedback TOP and input 2 from `thresh1`, Cross around 0.3. On the Feedback TOP, set Target TOP to the Cross TOP. Each frame the output becomes a mix of the old and the new. Follow it with a Threshold at 0.5. Lower Cross means steadier but more lag.
- Box average with Cache TOP plus Cache Select. Set `cache1` to hold 4 frames, add three Cache Select TOPs reading indices 1, 2 and 3, and feed all four into a Composite TOP with Operation Add, then a Threshold at 2 of 4 or 3 of 4, that is 0.5 or 0.75. This is a true majority vote with no trail.

Try the Feedback route first. It is two operators and the Feedback TOP is a pattern you will reuse. After the vote, keep one small Blur and Threshold pair for the remaining edge pinholes.

## Prompt 4 — 2026-10-23 05:28:19 UTC

### User:

How do I set cache to 4 frames? And cache select only allows index 0 and less (negatives)

----

### Claude:

Both are TouchDesigner conventions rather than mistakes:

- **Cache size** is the "Cache Size" parameter on the Cache TOP's first page. Set it to 4. The Cache TOP also has its own "Output Index" there, which is what `cache1` currently outputs.
- **Negative indices are correct.** In the Cache TOP, index 0 is the newest frame and negative numbers step back in time. So set the three Cache Select TOPs to -1, -2 and -3, with `cache1` itself at 0. Those four, added in a Composite TOP, are the last four frames.

If an index does not update, check that "Active" on the Cache TOP is on, and that it is not set to step only on a pulse.
